Welcome to our writing community

As your instructor, it is my pleasure to welcome you to English 102. This course is designed to introduce you to research-based writing and to assist you in further developing your writing skills.  I will act as your guide in those regards, supporting, encouraging, and facilitating your growth as the semester progresses. 

I invite you consider yourself a member of the academic community (because you are), one who can bring value to ongoing discussions about various topics that span a wide variety of perspectives, issues, and beliefs. Conducting research will help you create that value through exploring and better understanding a given topic /issue until you are sufficiently knowledgeable to offer additional insights into the matter at hand.

Course Information

*Please note that the syllabus and associated documents are subject to change if the need arises during the course of the semester.

Catalog Description

Students further develop the composition and comprehension skills they gained in Rhetoric I. The course focuses on writing as a means of learning to reason effectively. In the process of writing several academic essays, including a minimum of 10 pages of research writing, students learn to interweave their own ideas with those of their sources. Students also learn basic text-based and electronic research methods and proper documentation procedures. Lecture three hours per week.

Prerequisite

ENG 101 (Rhetoric I) with a grade of “C” or better. 

General Education Objectives

Rhetoric II addresses four general education objectives listed in the college catalog. Students will learn to:

  • communicate clearly, coherently, and accurately with varied audiences and for a range of purposes;
  • reason using a variety of analytical tools and processes in a wide array of contexts;
  • use ethical perspectives to make decisions; and
  • demonstrate academic or professional literacy within varied content areas.

Student Learning Outcomes

Learning outcomes are what you will be able to do when you pass the course. Successful students will:

  • demonstrate how context, purpose, and audience affect writing;
  • develop a reasonable position while accurately and responsibly integrating views different from their own;
  • find, identify, and evaluate source material for a research paper;
  • integrate source material using attribution, quotation, paraphrase, and summary;
  • use MLA or APA documentation properly; and
  • write a competent impromptu essay that uses sources effectively.

Required Grade of “C”

All entering freshmen (first-time college enrollees) must earn at least a “C” in each of ENG 101 and ENG 102 for satisfactory completion of the IAI General Education Package and for graduation from the College in all university transfer degrees (A.A., A.S., and A.F.A.). This is a regulation of the IAI Steering Panel and is endorsed by the Illinois Community College Board.

Evaluation & Grading

Graded Coursework

You will write three formal, graded essays this semester — two short research papers and one final research paper — totaling at least ten pages of research writing that uses sources and follows MLA documentation practices (in-text citations and a works cited page). You will also complete shorter informal writing, peer critiques, and a departmental final exam.

Requirements to Pass the Course

In addition to earning a passing weighted average, you must earn passing grades on both the final research paper and the departmental final exam to pass the course. A student whose weighted average is passing but who fails either of these two components will not pass. Remember also that a “C” or better is required for IAI transfer credit.

Evaluation Criteria

Assignment grades, and ultimately course grades, are determined by your ability to bring perspective and evidence to bear on an issue, recognize and respond to other points of view, read with comprehension, use Standard Edited English effectively, and — in at least one paper — follow the conventions for documenting research.

Guidelines for Papers

  • For all papers, follow the standard format for headings, titles, spacing and margins (as per instructor and handbook).
  • Follow MLA documentation practices (refer to the Blackboard link entitled “MLA Guidelines”).
  • Type or use a word processor for all assignments.
  • Give all papers an original title.

Final Exam

All students complete — and must pass — a departmental final exam during finals week. The exam is an impromptu essay that uses provided source articles; those articles are distributed and introduced in class beforehand (see the course calendar).

Course Policies

Attendance

This course meets in person regularly, and attendance is taken every class. Your attendance is directly linked to your performance, and you are expected to make every effort to be present and arrive ready to work.

  • Accumulating more than three unexcused absences will reduce your participation grade at the end of the course. Excessive tardiness may be treated the same way.
  • Excessive absences (more than three unexcused absences) may result in your being dropped from the course. 
  • An absence may be excused for documented illness, family emergency, religious observance, jury duty or military service, or a required college activity. To have an absence excused, contact your instructor before class when possible, and no later than one week after the absence, with documentation.
  • If you miss class for any reason, you are responsible for the material covered. Check Blackboard and consult with your instructor as soon as possible.

Participation

Your participation in our class sessions is one of the components of your final course grade. To earn full credit for participation: avoid excessive (more than three unexcused) absences or tardiness, contribute to some of our class discussions over the course of the semester, and participate in all peer critiques.

Peer Critique

Some of the essays you compose will be read and critiqued by one or more of your peers. The goal of peer critique is to give each writer additional feedback on a draft before the final draft is graded. This feedback may help you improve your work, but you are not responsible for helping your partner(s) earn a higher grade. Your earnest, consistent participation in peer critique is an important component of your participation grade.

Late Work

Timely submission of assignments is expected. Unless extenuating circumstances are documented and approved:

  • Work submitted up to one class meeting late will be reduced by one letter grade. 
  • Work submitted up to two class meetings late will be reduced by two letter grades. 
  • Work more than two class meetings late will not be accepted and will receive a grade of zero.

Documented extenuating circumstances (serious illness, family emergency, and the like) will be considered on a case-by-case basis. If you anticipate a problem meeting a deadline, talk to me before the due date whenever possible.

Electronic Devices

All electronic devices that are not directly related to instruction should be put away and placed in silent or vibrate mode. This will help to minimize distractions during class meetings. If you must take or make an urgent call, please step out of the classroom to do so.

Academic Honesty

Use of another person’s ideas, words or data as if they are one’s own, called plagiarism, is a serious academic offence. Plagiarism is claiming to have written something that is not your own: putting your name on any writing that you did not write yourself is plagiarism. A single incident may cause a student to fail the course and to face disciplinary action, as outlined in the Morton College Student Handbook, the Morton College Catalog and the Code of Student Conduct.

All papers handed in for a grade in this class must be the student’s own work and written for this class only. Reworking old papers, or using a paper from another class to satisfy requirements in this one, is not acceptable. A student who does either of these things without permission will be required to redo the assignment.

A Note on AI and Other Writing Technologies

You are expected to do your own writing. Having a friend, relative, or a machine (such as ChatGPT or another AI tool) write for you is not allowed. Beyond the honesty issue, outsourcing your writing means you won’t learn anything about how to write better — and that is the point of this class.

  • Using the “Grammar Check” or “Spell Check” function in your word processor is fine.
  • Using Grammarly to edit fluency and mechanical errors in your own written ideas is fine, but it’s not OK to use new ideas Grammarly “gave” you. Be especially cautious with generative features such as “Grammarly Go.”
  • If you are not sure whether your use of a technology is problematic, ask your instructor before submitting.
  • This class uses Turnitin, which includes tools intended to flag AI-generated writing. Because no detection tool is perfectly reliable, a flag by itself will not result in an automatic penalty; instead, it will prompt a conversation.
  • Your instructor reserves the right to ask you about the content, structure, and/or writing process of any paper submitted in this course, and may request impromptu in-class writing samples (handwritten or otherwise) to retain for their records. If, after this process, the instructor determines that a course-submitted essay was not authentically written by you, you will receive a grade of 0 on the assignment and/or fail the course, in line with the College’s Academic Honesty Policy.

How Your Essays Are Graded

My Perspective on Grading

Grading an essay you submit is really another opportunity for me to teach. What matters most to me when I grade your writing is whether you are growing as a writer over the course of our semester. This is why I provide audio feedback on your work: the feedback is designed to highlight your strengths and your opportunities for growth. Everything we do in this class is designed to contribute to that growth.

Essay Assignment Grading

You will be asked to complete several essay assignments over the course of the semester. Here’s an overview of the grading process:

  1. Complete the first draft of the essay.
  2. Share your first draft with a classmate and engage in peer critique.
  3. Revise your draft based on the feedback from the peer critique.
  4. Submit the final draft to your instructor via Blackboard.
  5. Your instructor provides feedback, posts it to Blackboard, and records your grade in the gradebook.
  6. If you earned a failing grade, revise according to the feedback and resubmit. If you earned a “C” or higher, review the feedback and apply it to the next assignment.

What if I fail an essay?

If you fail an essay assignment, you may revise and resubmit your paper once to try to earn a higher grade. If you earn a “C” or higher on the revised draft, that grade becomes the final grade for the assignment.

Please submit the best first draft possible: revision is available only to replace a failing grade, not to move from a “C” to a “B” or from a “B” to an “A.”
